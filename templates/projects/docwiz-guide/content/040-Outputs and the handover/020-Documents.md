# Documents

A **document** is the quiet sibling of a slide deck: one tidy page of sections for one audience. Use it for a help-desk quick reference, an administrator guide, or a checklist for site acceptance — anything that picks the relevant parts of the manual for one kind of reader.

This guide has two: the **Quick Reference** for authors and the **Administrator Guide** for administrators. Both are tabs in the handover.

## Create a document {#create-a-document}

On the **Outputs** tab, click **+ New output**, give it a **Title**, choose who it is **For**, and pick **Document (one page of sections)** under **Kind**.

A document is made of sections instead of slides. Each section has a heading and one content area, and most sections simply show a section of the manual — a linked section, exactly as on a slide (see [Show a section of the manual](dw:slide-decks#linked-sections)). Add your own short text where it helps the reader, for example a sentence that explains what the document is for.

- Click the **+** between two sections, or **+ Add section**, to add one.
- Drag sections in the column on the left to reorder them.
- Click **Read** in the output bar to see the finished document in a new tab.

## Filtered for its audience {#filtered}

A document shows its linked sections the way its audience should see them: blocks and steps tagged for other readers are left out. In the handover, each linked section ends with an **open in manual** link to the full chapter, for readers who want the whole story.

::: audience author
The *Quick Reference* in this guide shows this: the chapter about readers has a step only administrators see, and the author's document leaves it out.
:::

## Slide deck or document? {#deck-or-document}

The same output can be either: **Slides · Document** in the output bar switches it. As a rule of thumb:

- a **slide deck** is for presenting to a group — little text, large pictures;
- a **document** is for reading and looking things up — complete procedures and tables.
